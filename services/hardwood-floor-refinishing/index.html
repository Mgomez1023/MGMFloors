Service Overview

Understand the floor before choosing a refinishing plan

Professional hardwood floor refinishing begins with an evaluation of the existing wood, its condition, and the result you want to achieve.

Some visible wear may be concentrated in traffic paths while other concerns extend into the boards. An on-site review helps determine whether sanding and refinishing are appropriate.

When refinishing may be appropriate

  • The finish looks dull or worn through in traffic areas
  • Surface scratches and discoloration affect the overall appearance
  • A different stain tone or sheen is being considered
  • Existing hardwood remains suitable for another finishing cycle
  • Connected rooms need a more consistent appearance

The MGM Approach

A five-step approach

Floor sanding in Chicago should balance removal, color, and protection without assuming every floor needs the same treatment.

  1. 01

    Evaluate the floor

    Review wear, previous coatings, damaged areas, edges, and transitions.

  2. 02

    Confirm the direction

    Discuss natural tone, stain possibilities, sheen, and practical expectations.

  3. 03

    Prepare and sand

    Prepare the work area and sand the suitable wood surface progressively.

  4. 04

    Refine color

    Review the prepared wood and apply the selected stain approach when stain is part of the plan.

  5. 05

    Apply the finish

    Build the selected finish system and review edges and transitions.

Service Considerations

Sanding, stain, and finish decisions

The right sequence depends on the wood and existing condition—not only the desired color.

Natural or stained appearance

Leaving wood closer to its natural tone emphasizes species and grain. Stain changes the overall color, but samples can vary with wood, lighting, and preparation.

Finish and renewed-surface care

Sheen and finish selection affect appearance and use. After completion, follow the product-specific guidance provided for furniture, rugs, cleaning, and regular care.

Project Details

Details that shape a consistent result

Edges, transitions, repairs, and sample decisions deserve the same attention as the open floor.

Existing condition
Previous finishes, deep damage, and board thickness can affect what is practical.
Localized repairs
Boards needing repair should be identified before the finishing sequence.
Color samples
Samples help evaluate undertones, variation, and lighting in the actual room.
Edges and transitions
Perimeters, stairs, thresholds, and adjoining surfaces require coordinated detailing.
Surface protection
Care guidance should reflect the selected finish and conditions in the space.
