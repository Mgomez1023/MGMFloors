Service Overview

Start with the existing material, not a predetermined solution

Older floors may combine sound boards, localized damage, previous patches, worn finish, gaps, and decades of color change.

MGM evaluates those conditions before defining repair, selective replacement, sanding, staining, and refinishing work. The goal is a coherent floor that respects usable material and realistic limits.

Questions a restoration review should answer

  • Which original boards remain usable
  • Where damage requires repair or replacement
  • Whether sanding is appropriate for the floor
  • How old and new material may blend
  • Which character marks should remain visible

The MGM Approach

A five-step approach

Restoration decisions develop from the floor’s condition and the amount of original material that can reasonably remain.

  1. 01

    Document conditions

    Review wear, damage, patches, transitions, previous coatings, and visible character.

  2. 02

    Set priorities

    Identify material to preserve, areas to repair, and expectations for variation.

  3. 03

    Complete repairs

    Integrate suitable replacement material and address localized details where practical.

  4. 04

    Sand and refine

    Prepare appropriate surfaces and develop the selected natural or stained direction.

  5. 05

    Finish cohesively

    Apply the planned finish and review repaired areas, edges, and transitions together.

Service Considerations

Preservation and replacement in balance

Restoration is not the same as making old wood look newly manufactured.

Preserving usable material

Sound boards, natural variation, and age-related character can remain part of the floor when condition and project goals support preservation.

Replacing damaged areas

Replacement boards may differ in age, grain, or tone. Careful layout and finishing can help them belong without promising invisibility.

Project Details

Character, repairs, and refinishing limits

The strongest restoration plan distinguishes meaningful character from conditions that threaten the floor’s performance.

Floor history
Earlier patches, fasteners, coatings, and sanding can affect options.
Remaining wood
Construction and usable thickness matter before another sanding cycle.
Repair boundaries
Staggered board work can integrate more naturally than abrupt rectangular patches.
Color expectations
Older and replacement wood may respond differently to stain and finish.
Original character
Some variation, marks, and patina may remain intentionally visible.
