Service Overview

Color matching is a process of comparison, not a paint formula

Wood absorbs stain according to species, grain, preparation, and individual board character. The same sample can shift under daylight and artificial light.

MGM uses project samples and surrounding woodwork as references while setting realistic expectations: new and existing materials may coordinate closely without becoming identical.

What influences the final color

  • Wood species and natural undertones
  • Existing age, finish, and sun exposure
  • Sanding and surface preparation
  • Daylight and evening lighting
  • Cabinetry, trim, stairs, and nearby flooring

The MGM Approach

A five-step approach

Samples narrow the direction while the actual floor reveals how color behaves across grain and changing light.

  1. 01

    Gather references

    Review inspiration, existing woodwork, adjoining floors, and desired depth of color.

  2. 02

    Identify the wood

    Consider species, age, condition, and how preparation may affect absorption.

  3. 03

    Develop samples

    Compare practical color directions on representative wood or prepared project areas.

  4. 04

    Review in the room

    Look at undertones in multiple locations and lighting conditions.

  5. 05

    Confirm stain and sheen

    Coordinate the selected stain direction with the planned finish appearance.

Service Considerations

Undertones, variation, and realistic matching

A good match belongs with the surrounding interior even when natural materials prevent exact duplication.

Working with undertones

Brown, amber, red, gray, and neutral influences can become more noticeable beside cabinets, painted trim, and existing wood.

Understanding variation

Earlywood, latewood, grain direction, repairs, and board age can produce lighter and darker areas within the same approved stain direction.

Project Details

How to evaluate a stain sample

A small sample is useful, but it should be read as part of the room rather than in isolation.

Representative wood
Samples are most useful when the species and preparation relate to the project.
Multiple locations
Light changes across windows, corners, hallways, and connected rooms.
Existing woodwork
Coordination may be more realistic than exact replication across different materials.
Board variation
Evaluate the overall field, not only the lightest or darkest board.
Sheen selection
Finish sheen changes reflection and can alter how the color reads.
