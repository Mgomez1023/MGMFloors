Service Overview

Treat the staircase as connected architecture

Treads, risers, landings, nosings, posts, and transitions each contribute to the finished staircase.

MGM reviews existing conditions and how the stairs meet nearby flooring before recommending refinishing, repair, staining, or selective replacement work.

Stair details to evaluate

  • Wear concentrated along tread paths and edges
  • Damaged treads, nosings, or landing boards
  • Painted and natural-wood elements
  • Color differences between stairs and adjoining floors
  • Tight corners, turning treads, posts, and trim

The MGM Approach

A five-step approach

A step-by-step review helps coordinate repair, preparation, color, and finishing across varied stair components.

  1. 01

    Inspect the stairs

    Review treads, risers, landings, edges, movement, and adjoining floors.

  2. 02

    Define the scope

    Identify refinishing, repairs, replacements, and elements that will remain unchanged.

  3. 03

    Plan color

    Compare stair wood, nearby flooring, posts, railings, trim, and lighting.

  4. 04

    Prepare and refinish

    Complete the appropriate surface preparation, stain work, and finishing sequence.

  5. 05

    Review connections

    Check tread edges, landings, transitions, and visual continuity from floor to floor.

Service Considerations

Treads, risers, and adjoining floors

The stair is experienced close-up, making small inconsistencies more noticeable.

Existing components

Treads and landings may be different species, ages, or constructions. Painted risers and trim also shape preparation and color decisions.

Floor-to-stair coordination

Stairs can coordinate with adjoining floors without becoming an exact match when wood, age, or lighting differs. Samples help set direction.

Project Details

Where staircase craftsmanship shows

Repeated lines and edges make consistent detailing especially important.

Tread surfaces
Wear patterns and prior coatings affect the preparation plan.
Nosings and edges
Visible front edges need careful transitions and consistent color.
Risers and trim
Painted or wood elements should be defined clearly in the scope.
Turning steps
Winders and curved layouts require individual attention to grain and edges.
Landings
Floor direction and color should connect deliberately to the stair run.
