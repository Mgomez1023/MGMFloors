Service Overview

Match the flooring plan to how the space is used

Residential decisions often center on continuity, comfort, existing finishes, and daily routines. Business and managed-property work may place additional emphasis on access, occupants, traffic, and coordination.

MGM reviews the actual property and proposed material before defining installation, repair, refinishing, or restoration work.

Project conditions to discuss early

  • Occupied or vacant work areas
  • Building access and material movement
  • Daily traffic and expected use
  • Connections between rooms and flooring types
  • Coordination with owners, managers, or other trades

The MGM Approach

A five-step approach

Clear project information helps align the flooring scope with the property and people using it.

  1. 01

    Review the property

    Consider use, rooms, access, existing floors, transitions, and stakeholders.

  2. 02

    Define the scope

    Confirm materials, service needs, work areas, and decisions requiring site review.

  3. 03

    Coordinate details

    Plan layout, building access, adjoining work, and practical communication points.

  4. 04

    Complete the work

    Carry out the agreed flooring service with attention to edges, transitions, and occupied areas.

  5. 05

    Review the result

    Walk through the completed scope and identify care information relevant to the selected floor.

Service Considerations

Residential comfort and commercial coordination

The best specification responds to the setting rather than relying on a single material for every property.

Homes and residential interiors

Room flow, stairs, cabinetry, family routines, and transitions often guide layout and finish decisions.

Offices, retail, and managed properties

Access, traffic, maintenance expectations, occupants, and coordination may carry more weight. Product and site requirements still need individual review.

Project Details

Planning beyond the field of flooring

A complete scope considers how work reaches, moves through, and connects within the property.

Access
Entries, elevators, stairs, loading limitations, and building rules can affect planning.
Traffic
Expected use informs material and finish conversations without replacing product-specific guidance.
Layout
Board direction and transitions influence circulation and visual continuity.
Coordination
Other trades, occupants, and property contacts should be considered in the scope.
Care expectations
Cleaning and protection guidance should suit the selected product and finish.
