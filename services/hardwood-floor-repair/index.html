Service Overview

Repair the problem without losing sight of the whole floor

Damaged boards, open gaps, localized wear, and awkward transitions each require a different response.

MGM reviews the affected area, nearby material, floor movement, and access before recommending board replacement, patching, transition work, or a broader restoration plan.

Conditions worth evaluating

  • Boards with visible impact, moisture, or pet damage
  • Localized wear that differs sharply from nearby flooring
  • Gaps, movement, or loose areas
  • Poorly resolved thresholds and room transitions
  • Patches that need better visual integration

The MGM Approach

A five-step approach

Repair decisions are guided by the cause, available matching material, and realistic blending expectations.

  1. 01

    Inspect the area

    Review the damage, nearby boards, transitions, and possible contributing conditions.

  2. 02

    Define the repair

    Determine whether the work is localized or connected to a larger floor concern.

  3. 03

    Plan the patch

    Consider board size, species, grain, color, and how replacement pieces can be integrated.

  4. 04

    Complete the repair

    Remove and replace suitable material or refine the transition as the condition requires.

  5. 05

    Blend and review

    Coordinate sanding, color, or finish work when needed and review the repaired area in context.

Service Considerations

Matching material and blending repairs

A structurally sound patch and an invisible patch are not always the same promise.

Board and profile compatibility

Species, width, thickness, milling, grade, and age influence how a replacement board fits and looks beside existing material.

Color and finish integration

New wood and older flooring may accept color differently. Sampling and selective or broader refinishing may improve the transition, but variation can remain.

Project Details

What a careful repair considers

The repair area, its cause, and the surrounding finish all influence the practical scope.

Cause of damage
Recurring moisture or movement should be understood before cosmetic work.
Available material
Existing spare boards or compatible stock can affect matching options.
Patch layout
Board staggering and joint placement help a repair relate to the original installation.
Transition height
Adjoining flooring and thresholds need safe, deliberate connections.
Blending limits
Age, light exposure, and previous finish can make exact color matching unrealistic.
