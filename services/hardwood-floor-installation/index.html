A Floor Planned for the Whole Room

Thoughtful installation starts before the first board is placed

Hardwood installation is more than fastening planks. The direction of the boards, the rhythm of joints, the condition of the subfloor, and the way the new surface meets stairs, tile, cabinetry, and doorways all shape the result.

MGM approaches those decisions as one connected plan. We review the room and material, identify details that need attention, and install with a clean, consistent layout suited to the property.

When new hardwood installation is appropriate

  • New construction, additions, and extensively remodeled rooms
  • Spaces where the existing floor cannot reasonably be restored
  • Connecting rooms that need a more continuous visual flow
  • Replacing a different flooring material with real wood
  • Projects that require a new species, width, tone, or installation pattern

Material Considerations

Solid versus engineered hardwood

Both can produce a genuine wood floor. The better fit depends on the installation location, site conditions, construction, and the look you want to carry through the home or business.

Solid hardwood

Each board is milled from one piece of wood. It is often considered where the structure and installation conditions support a traditional solid-wood assembly.

  • Wide choice of species, grades, widths, and finish directions
  • Site-finished options can create a unified surface across boards
  • Subfloor construction and environmental conditions need careful review

Engineered hardwood

A real-wood wear layer is bonded over a layered core. Its construction can make it useful for situations where solid hardwood is not the preferred assembly.

  • Available in a broad range of factory-finished looks
  • Can suit several installation methods and project conditions
  • Product construction and wear-layer specifications vary by manufacturer

What Makes the Difference

Preparation, transitions, and finishing details

A strong visual result depends on decisions that are easy to overlook once furniture returns to the room.

Surface preparation
Irregularities and questionable areas should be identified before they can affect the new floor.
Board layout
Plank direction, starting lines, and joint distribution influence how balanced the room feels.
Transitions
Doorways, stairs, tile, and adjoining floors need deliberate connections rather than improvised endings.
Perimeter details
Edges around walls, trim, cabinetry, vents, and fixed elements contribute to a clean installation.
Finish coordination
For site-finished floors, color and sheen choices should relate to the wood, lighting, and nearby finishes.
